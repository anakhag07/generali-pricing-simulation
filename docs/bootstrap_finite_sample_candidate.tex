\section{Scope and the unchanged calibration rule}

This note addresses the ideal exact-arithmetic counterpart of the
homoskedastic Gaussian quadratic specialization of
\texttt{MATH.md} section 7.7. It keeps pairs resampling, full-rank redraws, the
original fixed standard-error denominator, and the supremum over the entire
real line. Rank acceptance here means exact algebraic rank. The Python
implementation uses tolerance-based \texttt{numpy.linalg.matrix\_rank};
its accept/reject decisions are covered only if they agree with exact rank.
In particular, this theorem does not account for additional numerical
rejections of algebraically full-rank resamples. No new optimizer, objective,
random process, or experiment is introduced. The heteroskedastic GP profile
is outside this theorem.

Fix distinct actions $a_1,\ldots,a_n\in\R$, with $n\ge5$, and write
\[
 p(a)=(1,a,a^2)^\top,\qquad P_{i\cdot}=p(a_i)^\top,
 \qquad V=(P^\top P)^{-1}.
\]
The design has rank three. Conditional on this entire design, assume
\[
 Y=P\beta_0+\sigma z,\qquad z\sim N(0,I_n),\qquad \sigma>0.
\]
Thus $f(a)=p(a)^\top\beta_0$ for every real $a$. Let
\[
 \widetilde\beta=VP^\top Y,\quad
 \widetilde f(a)=p(a)^\top\widetilde\beta,\quad
 \widehat\sigma^2=\frac{\|Y-P\widetilde\beta\|^2}{n-3},\quad
 s(a)=\widehat\sigma\sqrt{p(a)^\top Vp(a)}.
\]
The residual scale is positive almost surely. The frozen predictor
$\widehat f:\R\to\R$ need not be quadratic or correctly specified.

Each bootstrap count vector $W=(W_1,\ldots,W_n)$ has the
$\operatorname{Multinomial}(n;1/n,\ldots,1/n)$ law conditioned on the resampled
design having rank three. Distinct bootstrap replicates are independent,
conditional on the observations. Each is refitted by ordinary least squares.
Set
\[
 T_b^*=\sup_{a\in\R}
 \frac{|p(a)^\top(\widetilde\beta_b^*-\widetilde\beta)|}{s(a)}.
\]
Assume the certified upper endpoints $T_b^+\ge T_b^*$ are measurable functions
of the observations and bootstrap randomness. The code sets $c$ to
their $k$th order statistic, where, for $p_0=1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank}
 \Prob\{\operatorname{Binomial}(B,p_0)\ge k\}\le\eta,
 \qquad 1\le k\le B.
\end{equation}
This is the existing \texttt{mc\_upper} rule; a choice of $B$ for which no such
$k$ exists must be rejected. Define
\[
 w(a)=c\,s(a),\qquad
 r(a)=|\widehat f(a)-\widetilde f(a)|+w(a).
\]
All probability claims below concern ideal exact-arithmetic OLS, positive
scale arithmetic, exact-rank acceptance, and valid measurable upper endpoints.
They do not cover floating-point fit error or certify Python's numerical
rank decisions.

\section{An explicit bound}

Let $Q=PV^{1/2}$, so $Q^\top Q=I_3$, and let $q_i^\top$ be its rows.
Define leverage quantities
\[
 h_i=\|q_i\|^2,\qquad L=\sum_{i=1}^n h_i^2,
 \qquad h=\max_i h_i.
\]
In particular $9/n\le L\le3h\le3$. Define
\begin{align*}
 \alpha&=L-3/n,\\
 \gamma&=(3-10/n+8/n^2)L
             +3(-1/n+6/n^2-6/n^3),\\
 \zeta&=(1-2/n)L+3/n^2,\\
 q_{\mathrm{sing}}
   &=\frac{n+\binom n2(2^n-2)}{n^n},\qquad
 r_{\mathrm{acc}}=1-q_{\mathrm{sing}}.
\end{align*}
These three moment quantities are nonnegative. For any fixed $a\in(0,1)$
(a stability parameter, not an action), put
\begin{align}
 d_a&=\sqrt\gamma+\frac{\sqrt3}{n}
                       +2(1+a)\sqrt\alpha,\nonumber\\
 e_a&=\sqrt\zeta+\alpha,\nonumber\\
 \varepsilon_a
 &=\min\left\{1,
       \frac{2\alpha}{a^2r_{\mathrm{acc}}^2}
       +\frac{\sqrt{2d_a^2+2e_a^2}}
         {(1-a)^2r_{\mathrm{acc}}}\right\}.
 \label{eq:epsilon}
\end{align}
There is no unknown population constant in this expression.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-sample coverage; Lean verified]\label{thm:main}
Under the preceding assumptions, for every deterministic
$0<\tau<1-\delta$,
\begin{align}
 &\Prob\left\{
   |\widetilde f(a)-f(a)|\le w(a)\text{ for every }a\in\R
   \,\middle|\, P\right\}\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{2cm}\ge
 1-\delta-\tau-\eta-\frac{\varepsilon_a}{\tau^2}.
 \label{eq:coverage}
\end{align}
The same lower bound holds for
$|\widehat f(a)-f(a)|\le r(a)$ simultaneously over all real actions.
On that same event the excess radius also satisfies
\[
 0\le r(u)-|\widehat f(u)-f(u)|\le2w(u)\qquad\text{for every }u\in\R.
\]
The bound is clipped below at zero if desired. The probability integrates
over hypothetical observation noise and the bootstrap randomness; it is not
a posterior probability conditional on the already observed response vector.
\end{theorem}

For example, $a=1/2$ gives the simpler valid upper bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simple}
 \varepsilon_{1/2}\le
 \min\left\{1,
       \frac{8L+33\sqrt L}{r_{\mathrm{acc}}^2}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The proof below supplies the approximation bound rather than assuming
bootstrap validity. For a sequence of fixed designs with $L\to0$,
$\varepsilon_{1/2}=O(\sqrt L)$; selecting a deterministic positive
$\tau$ of order $\varepsilon_{1/2}^{1/3}$ yields a coverage loss
$O(L^{1/6})+\eta$. For the nominal limiting lower bound $1-\delta$, also take
$\eta=\eta_n\to0$ and choose $B_n$ so that \eqref{eq:rank} remains feasible.
For example, $(1-\delta)^{B_n}\le\eta_n$ permits at least $k=B_n$.
This is a deliberately conservative rate; feasibility alone does not establish
efficiency of the finite-$B_n$ quantile choice.
Equation~\eqref{eq:simple} and this asymptotic-rate discussion are analytic
corollaries in this note; the final Lean theorem uses \eqref{eq:epsilon}
directly and does not formalize those corollaries.

\section{Proof of the theorem}

\subsection{Whitening and cancellation of the fixed scale}

Let $R=I_n-QQ^\top$, and introduce the norm
\[
 g(v)=\sup_{u\in\R}
 \frac{|p(u)^\top V^{1/2}v|}{\sqrt{p(u)^\top Vp(u)}}
 \quad(v\in\R^3).
\]
Every vector inside the supremum has Euclidean norm one, so $g(v)\le\|v\|$.
Three distinct evaluation actions span the quadratic feature space, hence
$g$ is a genuine norm. Its unit ball is bounded and contains a neighborhood
of zero. Consequently $F(t)=\Prob\{g(Z)\le t\}$ for $Z\sim N(0,I_3)$ is
continuous, strictly increasing on $t\ge0$, with $F(0)=0$ and
$F(t)\to1$. One justification is to use polar coordinates: conditional on
a Gaussian direction, $g(Z)$ is its positive directional scale multiplied
by a radial random variable with positive continuous density on $(0,\infty)$.

For an unconditioned multinomial count vector, use the same letter $W$ for
its diagonal matrix and set
\[
 A_W=Q^\top WQ,\quad E_W=A_W-I_3,\quad
 B_W=Q^\top(W-I_n)^2Q.
\]
Whenever $A_W$ is invertible, the pairs-refit identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refit}
 \frac{V^{-1/2}(\widetilde\beta^*_W-\widetilde\beta)}{\sigma}
   =G_W:=A_W^{-1}Q^\top(W-I_n)Rz.
\end{equation}
Indeed the response residual is $\sigma Rz$, and the weighted normal
equations give this identity directly. Also
$V^{-1/2}(\widetilde\beta-\beta_0)/\sigma=Q^\top z\sim N(0,I_3)$.
Writing $\lambda=\widehat\sigma/\sigma>0$ gives
\[
 T_W^*=g(G_W)/\lambda,\qquad
 \sup_u|\widetilde f(u)-f(u)|/s(u)=g(Q^\top z)/\lambda.
\]
Thus with $\widehat q=\lambda c$, the auxiliary coverage event is exactly
$g(Q^\top z)\le\widehat q$. Multiplication of all certified upper endpoints
by the same positive $\lambda$ preserves their order and conservativeness.
The Gaussian vectors $Q^\top z$ and $Rz$ are independent, although the
single-threshold union bound below does not require this independence.

\subsection{Exact multinomial moments}

Take independent unconditioned count vectors $W,U$, and let
\[
 C_{WU}=Q^\top(W-I_n)(U-I_n)Q.
\]
The following identities hold:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
 \E\|E_W\|_F^2=\alpha,\quad
 \E\|B_W-(1-1/n)I_3\|_F^2=\gamma,\quad
 \E\|C_{WU}\|_F^2=\zeta.
\end{equation}
To check them, put $\xi_i=W_i-1$. Multinomial factorial moments give
\begin{align*}
 \E\xi_i\xi_j&=\mathbf1_{i=j}-1/n,\\
 \operatorname{Var}(\xi_i^2)&=3-11/n+14/n^2-6/n^3,\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(\xi_i^2,\xi_j^2)
    &=-1/n+6/n^2-6/n^3\quad(i\ne j).
\end{align*}
For completeness,
$\E\xi_i^4=4-13/n+15/n^2-6/n^3$ and
$\E\xi_i^2\xi_j^2=1-3/n+7/n^2-6/n^3$ for $i\ne j$.
Expand each squared Frobenius norm as a double sum weighted by
$(q_i^\top q_j)^2$ and use
\[
 \sum_i\|q_i\|^4=L,\qquad
 \sum_{i,j}(q_i^\top q_j)^2=3.
\]
For $C_{WU}$, independence squares the covariance
$\mathbf1_{i=j}-1/n$, giving the displayed $\zeta$.
Independence also gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product}
 \E\|E_WE_U\|_F^2
 \le\E(\|E_W\|_F^2\|E_U\|_F^2)=\alpha^2.
\end{equation}

\subsection{A Gaussian comparison on stable resamples}

Let $\mathcal S$ be the event
$\|E_W\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le a$ and
$\|E_U\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le a$.
Then both resampled designs have full rank and
$\|A_W^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|A_U^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le(1-a)^{-1}$.
By Markov's inequality and \eqref{eq:moments},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unstable}
 \Prob(\mathcal S^c)\le2\alpha/a^2.
\end{equation}
Conditional on $W,U$, the six-dimensional vector $(G_W,G_U)$ is centered
Gaussian. Its diagonal covariance blocks and cross block are
\[
 H_W=A_W^{-1}(B_W-E_W^2)A_W^{-1},\quad
 H_U=A_U^{-1}(B_U-E_U^2)A_U^{-1},
\]
\[
 K_{WU}=A_W^{-1}(C_{WU}-E_WE_U)A_U^{-1}.
\]
In particular
\[
 H_W-I_3=A_W^{-1}
  \bigl[B_W-(1-1/n)I_3-I_3/n-2E_W-2E_W^2\bigr]A_W^{-1}.
\]
On $\mathcal S$, $\|E_W^2\|_F\le a\|E_W\|_F$. Minkowski's inequality in
$L^2$, followed by \eqref{eq:moments}--\eqref{eq:product}, therefore gives
\[
 \E[\mathbf1_{\mathcal S}\|H_W-I_3\|_F^2]
    \le\frac{d_a^2}{(1-a)^4},\qquad
 \E[\mathbf1_{\mathcal S}\|K_{WU}\|_F^2]
    \le\frac{e_a^2}{(1-a)^4}.
\]
The same diagonal bound holds for $U$. If $\Sigma_{WU}$ denotes the whole
six-dimensional covariance matrix, its block structure yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jointmoment}
 \E[\mathbf1_{\mathcal S}\|\Sigma_{WU}-I_6\|_F^2]
    \le\frac{2d_a^2+2e_a^2}{(1-a)^4}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Elementary Gaussian total-variation bound]\label{lem:tv}
For every positive semidefinite $m\times m$ matrix $\Sigma$,
\[
 \TV(N(0,\Sigma),N(0,I_m))\le\min\{1,\|\Sigma-I_m\|_F\},
\]
where $\TV(\mu,\nu)=\sup_A|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $\Sigma$ is singular, its Frobenius distance from the identity is at
least one, so the assertion is trivial. Otherwise diagonalize it with
positive eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m$. Direct Gaussian integration
gives the squared affinity of the two densities:
\[
 \mathcal A^2=\left(\int\sqrt{f_\Sigma f_I}\right)^2
          =\prod_j\frac{2\sqrt{\lambda_j}}{1+\lambda_j}.
\]
Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to
$|f_\Sigma-f_I|=|\sqrt{f_\Sigma}-\sqrt{f_I}|
(\sqrt{f_\Sigma}+\sqrt{f_I})$ gives
$\TV^2\le1-\mathcal A^2$. Since all product factors lie in $[0,1]$,
\begin{align*}
 1-\mathcal A^2
 &\le\sum_j\frac{(\sqrt{\lambda_j}-1)^2}{1+\lambda_j}\\
 &=\sum_j\frac{(\lambda_j-1)^2}
                  {(1+\lambda_j)(1+\sqrt{\lambda_j})^2}
 \le\sum_j(\lambda_j-1)^2=\|\Sigma-I_m\|_F^2.
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

For distinct actions, a quadratic resampled design is singular exactly when
its $n$ draws contain at most two different row indices. Counting the
sequences with one index, then those with exactly two, proves the stated
$q_{\mathrm{sing}}$. The probability that both $W,U$ are accepted is
$r_{\mathrm{acc}}^2$. Conditioning independent draws on their respective
acceptance events gives the required independent accepted-bootstrap law.

Apply convexity of total variation to the Gaussian mixture over accepted
$W,U$. On $\mathcal S^c$ use the bound one; on $\mathcal S$ apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:tv}. The first contribution is at most
$2\alpha/(a^2r_{\mathrm{acc}}^2)$ by \eqref{eq:unstable}. Cauchy--Schwarz
under the conditional accepted law and \eqref{eq:jointmoment} bound the
second by
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{2d_a^2+2e_a^2}}{(1-a)^2r_{\mathrm{acc}}}.
\]
Consequently the unconditional-over-noise joint law of the two accepted
bootstrap perturbations satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixturetv}
 \TV\bigl(\mathcal L(G_W,G_U),N(0,I_3)\otimes N(0,I_3)\bigr)
       \le\varepsilon_a.
\end{equation}

For \eqref{eq:simple}, $n\ge5$ implies
$\alpha\le L$, $\gamma\le3L$, and $\zeta\le L$. The bound for $\gamma$
uses $-1/n+6/n^2-6/n^3\le0$ for $n\ge5$ and its diagonal variance at most
three. Since $9/n\le L\le3$,
\[
 d_{1/2}\le(\sqrt3+3+1/\sqrt{15})\sqrt L<5\sqrt L,
 \qquad e_{1/2}\le(1+\sqrt3)\sqrt L<3\sqrt L.
\]
Therefore $4\sqrt{2d_{1/2}^2+2e_{1/2}^2}\le33\sqrt L$, since
$16\cdot68=1088<1089=33^2$. Finally $r_{\mathrm{acc}}\le1$ gives
\eqref{eq:simple}.

\subsection{From joint comparison to quantile coverage}

For fixed residual noise $Rz$, let
$H_z(t)=\Prob_W\{g(G_W)\le t\mid Rz\}$ under the accepted law. For each
deterministic $t$, \eqref{eq:mixturetv} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cdfmoment}
 \E_z[(H_z(t)-F(t))^2]\le\varepsilon_a.
\end{equation}
To see the constant explicitly, apply the total-variation bound to
\[
 (\mathbf1_{\{g(G_W)\le t\}}-F(t))
 (\mathbf1_{\{g(G_U)\le t\}}-F(t)).
\]
Its range has length $\max\{F(t),1-F(t)\}\le1$. Under two independent
standard Gaussians its expectation is zero. Under the shared residual
mixture, conditional independence of $W,U$ identifies its expectation with
the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:cdfmoment}.

The usual order-statistic argument, valid also for distributions with atoms,
and \eqref{eq:rank} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mc}
 \Prob\{H_z(\widehat q)<p_0\mid Rz\}\le\eta.
\end{equation}
For clarity, if $X$ has CDF $H$, then
$\Prob\{H(X)<p_0\}\le p_0$. An order statistic whose CDF value is less
than $p_0$ needs at least $k$ such observations; binomial monotonicity proves
\eqref{eq:mc}. Increasing sample statistics to their certified upper endpoints
can only decrease this failure probability.

Choose the deterministic $t_0$ satisfying $F(t_0)=p_0-\tau$.
If $\widehat q<t_0$ and $H_z(\widehat q)\ge p_0$, monotonicity implies
$H_z(t_0)-F(t_0)\ge\tau$. Hence \eqref{eq:cdfmoment}, Markov's inequality,
and \eqref{eq:mc} imply
\[
 \Prob\{\widehat q<t_0\}\le\eta+\varepsilon_a/\tau^2.
\]
A final union bound gives
\begin{align*}
 \Prob\{g(Q^\top z)\le\widehat q\}
 &\ge\Prob\{g(Q^\top z)\le t_0\}-\Prob\{\widehat q<t_0\}\\
 &\ge p_0-\tau-\eta-\varepsilon_a/\tau^2.
\end{align*}
This proves the auxiliary statement. On the same event, the triangle
inequality proves the transferred statement for every real action.
It also proves the useful deterministic refinement
\[
 0\le r(u)-|\widehat f(u)-f(u)|\le2w(u)\qquad(u\in\R).
\]
Indeed the upper bound follows from the reverse triangle inequality applied
to the two errors centered at $\widehat f(u)$.

\section{Rank retries, conditioning, and practical limits}

If the code permits at most $K$ redraws after its initial draw, each
replicate makes at most $K+1$ independent attempts. Conditional on successful
termination, the accepted law is exactly the rank-conditioned law above;
conditioning all replicates on successful termination preserves their
independence and does not condition the observation noise. Thus
\eqref{eq:coverage} applies conditional on successful termination.
Alternatively, counting an abort as failure gives
\[
 \Prob\{\text{procedure succeeds and the band covers}\mid P\}
 \ge1-\delta-\tau-\eta-\varepsilon_a/\tau^2
                      -Bq_{\mathrm{sing}}^{K+1}.
\]
The bound for abort probability is the union bound over replicates.
Lean proves the individual first-accepted return law and retry-failure
probability. The displayed batch-abort correction is not assembled into
the main formal theorem, whose resamples have the accepted law directly.
Pseudorandom generators are modeled here as the specified independent random
draws; their deterministic implementation is not a probability proof.

If actions themselves are random and almost surely distinct, the theorem
holds conditionally on every eligible design. It can be integrated over that
design distribution. A deterministic unconditional rate requires a separate
bound on $L$ or $h$; it must not silently replace the random design quantity
by its mean inside a nonlinear bound. Reusing observations across prefixes
does not change the marginal guarantee for a selected deterministic $n$;
simultaneous claims across many prefixes require an additional argument.

\texttt{ObservedCalibration.lean}, in the namespace

\begin{quote}\small

\end{quote}
The second version requires score certificates only for accepted resamples;
\texttt{AcceptedEndpoints.lean} proves a harmless zero extension on rejected

\section{Bounded originality comparison after Lean verification}

After the complete theorem compiled and its assumptions and axioms were
audited, a targeted comparison revisited the following primary sources.
The closest conceptual predecessor is the two-replicate $L^2$ argument of
B\"ucher and Kojadinovic~\cite{bk}. Our calculation is a quantitative
specialization of that known mechanism. Regression bootstrap validity,
nonasymptotic resampling bounds, Gaussian covariance comparison, and explicit
Monte Carlo error accounting also have established predecessors below.

The potentially useful contribution is the assembled, explicit leverage-based
bound for this exact-rank quadratic pairs rule, including its real-line norm,
fixed-denominator cancellation, exact-rank conditioning, finite $B$, and transfer to
a frozen predictor. This bounded search has not established that the assembled
result is original. No improvement in rate or assumptions is claimed, and the
current constants are vacuous for the archived designs. The continuum
supremum, fixed-scale cancellation, predictor transfer, and binomial
order-statistic guarantee are not independent novelty claims. A defensible
description is a formally verified finite-sample specialization for exact-rank
quadratic pairs-bootstrap calibration using established two-replica and
Gaussian-comparison methods. The exact assembled theorem was not located in
the checked results; this is not a priority or exhaustive-literature claim.

on a fixed quadratic design and gives explicit finite-$n$, finite-$B$

\bibitem{bk}
A. B\"ucher and I. Kojadinovic (2019),
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.01031}{\emph{A note on conditional versus joint
unconditional weak convergence in bootstrap consistency results}}.
Lemma 2.3 and its proof use two conditional replicas and an $L^2$ product
identity; Lemma 4.2 treats Monte Carlo quantiles. Their result is asymptotic
and traces the mechanism to earlier work.

\bibitem{abr}
S. Arlot, G. Blanchard and E. Roquain (2010),
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.0775}{\emph{Some nonasymptotic results on
resampling in high dimension, I: Confidence regions}}.
Theorem 2.1 treats resampling bounds for Gaussian vector means; Theorem 3.2
uses Rademacher quantiles; Proposition 3.4 explicitly controls empirical
quantiles with finitely many replicates. These are not pairs-refitted OLS bands.

\bibitem{sz}
V. Spokoiny and M. Zhilova (2015),
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.0347}{\emph{Bootstrap confidence sets under model
misspecification}}.
Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3 give nonasymptotic coverage errors for
multiplier-bootstrap likelihood regions under a small modeling bias;
they already establish finite-sample bootstrap validity in a different setting.
Their multipliers are independent, whereas our accepted multinomial counts
are dependent and enter a random inverse Gram matrix.

\bibitem{cck}
V. Chernozhukov, D. Chetverikov and K. Kato (2016),
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.00352}{\emph{Empirical and multiplier bootstraps
for suprema of empirical processes of increasing complexity}}.
Theorem 2.3 supplies nonasymptotic coupling for empirical-bootstrap suprema
under VC-type and moment conditions. A continuum supremum alone is therefore
not novel; our argument instead exploits exact Gaussian regression structure.

\bibitem{dmr}
L. Devroye, A. Mehrabian and T. Reddad,
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08693}{\emph{The total variation distance between
high-dimensional Gaussians with the same mean}} (2023 preprint version).
Theorem 1.1 compares Gaussian total variation with relative covariance
eigenvalues; Section 2 gives affinity/Hellinger bounds. Our elementary
Frobenius inequality follows from that standard affinity formula.
